# Wound Infection Data for Game Design: Historical Mortality & Outcomes

## 1. Sepsis Mortality (Pre-Antibiotic Era)

### Pre-modern / Pre-antibiotic periods
- **General sepsis mortality**: ~30% across all eras, but significantly higher in pre-antibiotic battlefield conditions
- **Severe sepsis mortality**: ~50% even with modern treatment; likely 70-90%+ without antibiotics in historical settings
- **Septic shock mortality**: ~80% (with modern ICU care); effectively near-certain death before antibiotics

### Modern statistics for comparison (pre-antibiotic baseline)
- In 2017, sepsis affected ~49 million people worldwide, causing ~11 million deaths (~1 in 5 global deaths)
- Mortality: sepsis ~30%, severe sepsis ~50%, septic shock ~80% (source: Sepsis Wikipedia / WHO data)

## 2. Battlefield Wound Infection Rates - Historical Data

### Napoleonic Wars (1803-1815)
**Primary Source: Wikipedia - Amputation article, section on Warfare**

Key statistics from the Napoleera era:
- **Gunshot wounds**: ~75% of all battlefield injuries
- **Amputation rate for gunshot limb wounds**: Very high; most surgeons amputated within 24 hours
- **Mortality after amputation**:
  - Before antiseptic practices (early Napoleonic): Estimated **30-50%** mortality from post-operative infection/sepsis
  - This means of every 100 soldiers who had a limb amputated, approximately 30-50 would die from wound complications
- **"Laudable pus" era**: Wound suppuration (pus formation) was considered normal/healing; this belief system meant infected wounds were NOT distinguished from healing ones

### American Civil War (1861-1865) - Pre-Antiseptic
**Primary Source: History of Surgery Wikipedia, Amputation article**

Key statistics:
- **Gunshot wounds**: ~75% of all battlefield injuries
- **Amputations performed**: ~30,000 out of ~60,000 surgical procedures (roughly 50% of surgeries)
- **Overall mortality rate after amputation**: **26%** for minor amputations (fingers/toes), rising to **45-50%** for major limb amputations
- **Mortality by wound type** (pre-antiseptic):
  - Simple fractures: ~17% mortality
  - Complicated fractures with bone damage: **~39-45%** mortality  
  - Gunshot wounds to joints: **~50-60%** mortality
- **"Carbolic acid" introduced late in the war**: Some evidence of slightly improved outcomes when antiseptic methods were adopted, but not widespread

### World War I (1914-1918) - Transition Period
**Primary Source: Amputation article section on Warfare**

Key statistics:
- **Gas gangrene (Clostridium perfringens)**: Major killer before antiseptic techniques
  - Mortality from gas gangrene without treatment: **~60-90%**
  - With Hughson's method (early debridement): Reduced to ~25%
  - With Carrel-Dakin antiseptic irrigation: Further reduced but still significant
- **Overall wound mortality**: ~15-20% of wounded died from wounds (not combat deaths)
- **Antiseptic adoption** (Carrel-Dakin method, 1915): Reduced sepsis rates significantly compared to pre-WWI era

### Key Infection Progression Timeline (Untreated Wound)
Based on surgical history literature:
1. **Day 0**: Wound sustained
2. **Day 1-3**: Initial inflammation; bacterial colonization begins
3. **Day 3-7**: Suppuration (pus formation); "laudable pus" era believed this meant healing
4. **Day 5-10**: Cellulitis spreads; systemic symptoms begin if uncontrolled
5. **Day 7-14**: Sepsis develops in severe cases; fever, confusion, tachycardia
6. **Day 10-21**: Septic shock or death OR gradual healing (if immune system wins)

### Factors Increasing Mortality (All Historical Periods)
- **Delay >24 hours** before amputation: Doubled mortality rates
- **Joint involvement**: 50-60% mortality vs. 30-40% for shaft fractures
- **Poor nutrition**: Significantly increased infection risk in military populations
- **Concomitant injuries**: Multiple wounds = compounding mortality
- **Age**: Older soldiers had significantly higher mortality across all eras

## 4. Galenic Theory of Wound Healing & "Laudable Pus"

### Core Beliefs (Ancient through Early Modern Period)

**Source: Hippocrates, Galen, and subsequent medieval/renaissance medical texts**

#### The Humoral Framework
- Four humors: blood, phlegm, yellow bile, black bile
- Disease = imbalance of humors
- Wounds disrupted local humor balance; healing required restoration

#### "Laudable Pus" (Pus Bonum et Laudabile)
This was the dominant medical belief from antiquity through the 17th century:

**Core tenet**: Pus formation in wounds was considered a **necessary and positive sign** of healing, not infection.

Key principles:
- Wounds should "suppurate" (form pus) to heal properly
- Clear/watery wound discharge ("sanious" wounds) was considered DANGEROUS - the wound was "cold" and needed warming/pus-producing treatment
- Thick white/yellow pus = good healing progression
- Dark, foul-smelling pus = bad prognosis but still part of expected process

**Hippocratic sources**: The Hippocratic Corpus described wound drainage as part of natural healing. Clean water or wine were recommended for wound cleansing (effective mild antiseptics).

**Galenic contribution**: Galen (c. 129-216 CE) codified these ideas into a system that dominated Western medicine for 1,300+ years:
- Wound healing required "coction" - a cooking/ripening process where humors in the wound reached proper state through heat
- Pus = cocted (cooked/ripe) material ready to be expelled
- Intervening too early (preventing pus) was thought to trap disease internally

**Practical consequences**:
- Surgeons deliberately PROMOTED suppuration with poultices and irritants
- Wounds that didn't produce pus were treated more aggressively to induce it
- This meant **infected wounds were indistinguishable from healing ones** - no concept of pathological infection vs. normal healing

### When Did This Change?
- **Hugh of Lucca (1150-1257)**: First recorded rejection of "laudable pus" theory; advocated dry wound treatment with wine and clean dressings at the School of Salerno
- **Ambroise Pare (1510-1590)**: French army surgeon who accidentally discovered that egg yolk/rose oil/turpentine dressing caused LESS pain and fever than boiling oil treatment for gunshot wounds; began transitioning away from pus-promotion
- **17th century**: Gradual shift as observation challenged dogma
- **1840s**: Semmelweis demonstrated handwashing reduced puerperal fever
- **1867**: Lister published antiseptic surgery principles - END of "laudable pus" era in practice
- **Post-1867**: The belief persisted among some practitioners well into the 1880s

## 5. Effective Antimicrobial Substances in Pre-Modern Medicine

### Historically Documented Antimicrobials

#### Honey
- **Effectiveness**: Well-documented antimicrobial properties (high osmolarity, hydrogen peroxide, low pH)
- **Historical use**: 
  - Edwin Smith Papyrus (~1600 BCE): "treat with honey every day" for wounds
  - Hippocratic corpus: honey and vinegar mixtures prescribed
  - Used continuously across all cultures through present day
  - Modern studies confirm effectiveness against many wound pathogens including MRSA
- **Game relevance**: Highly effective natural antiseptic; should significantly reduce infection risk

#### Vinegar (Acetic Acid)
- **Effectiveness**: Moderate antimicrobial activity
- **Historical use**: 
  - Hippocrates: honey + vinegar mixtures for wounds
  - Medieval period: diluted vinegar used for wound cleansing
  - WWI: vinegar solutions sometimes used as antiseptic irrigation
- **Game relevance**: Mildly effective; better than nothing

#### Wine/Alcohol
- **Effectiveness**: Moderate (70% ethanol optimal); historical wine was ~10-15% alcohol
- **Historical use**: 
  - Hippocrates: clean water OR wine for wound cleansing ("dry" treatment preferred)
  - Universal practice across all eras to flush wounds
  - Boiled wine also used (reduces pathogens, maintains antimicrobial properties)
- **Game relevance**: Commonly available; reduces but doesn't eliminate infection risk

#### Herbs with Documented Antimicrobial Activity

| Herb | Active Compound | Historical Use Period | Effectiveness |
|------|----------------|----------------------|---------------|
| Garlic (Allium sativum) | Allicin | Ancient Egypt, Greece, Medieval Europe | Moderate; documented antibacterial |
| Willow bark | Salicin (aspirin precursor) | Hippocratic era through 19th century | Anti-inflammatory/analgesic only; no antimicrobial |
| Poppy/Saffron | Opioid alkaloids | All periods | Analgesic only; no antimicrobial |
| Myrrh | Terpenes | Ancient Egypt, Medieval period | Moderate antiseptic when applied topically |
| Frankincense | Boswellic acids | Ancient/Medieval | Mild anti-inflammatory |
| Thyme | Thymol | Medieval European herbalism | Moderate antimicrobial |
| Rosemary | Carnosic acid | Medieval through Renaissance | Mild antimicrobial |
| Plantain (Plantago spp.) | Multiple compounds | Universal folk medicine | Moderate; draws out infection, mild antiseptic |

#### Gold
- **Historical use**: 2300-year-old mummy in Berlin has gold jewelry embedded in wound sites - suggests belief in antimicrobial properties
- **Effectiveness**: Noble metal; actually does resist bacterial colonization (oligodynamic effect)
- **Game relevance**: Could be represented as a rare, effective wound treatment

#### Copper and Silver
- **Historical use**: 
  - Copper vessels for water storage mentioned in Ebers Papyrus (~1550 BCE)
  - Silver nitrate used medicinally from medieval period onward
- **Effectiveness**: Strong oligodynamic antimicrobial effect; documented historically

### What Was NOT Effective (Common Misconceptions)
- **Mandrake**: Used as anesthetic but minimal antimicrobial value
- **Most religious/spiritual remedies**: No antimicrobial activity, though stress reduction may indirectly aid healing
- **"Drying out" wounds** (post-Hugh of Lucca approach): Can actually impede healing; clean moist environment is superior

## 6. Infection Rate Estimates for Game Design

### Estimated Infection Progression by Treatment Level

| Treatment Quality | Infection Rate | Septic Mortality (if infected) | Overall Survival |
|------------------|---------------|-------------------------------|-----------------|
| **None** (battlefield, untreated) | 60-80% of wounds become infected | ~70-90% die from sepsis | 25-40% overall wound survival |
| **Basic cleanliness** (clean water/wine rinse only) | 40-60% infection rate | ~50-70% die from sepsis | 40-55% overall wound survival |
| **Good care** (honey + wine + clean dressings, rest) | 20-35% infection rate | ~40-60% die from sepsis | 55-70% overall wound survival |
| **Excellent care** (herbal antiseptics + nutrition + isolation) | 10-20% infection rate | ~30-50% die from sepsis | 70-85% overall wound survival |
| **Antiseptic era** (carbolic acid/sterile techniques, post-1867) | 5-15% infection rate | ~20-40% die from sepsis | 80-90%+ overall wound survival |

### By Wound Severity (Untreated/Baseline)

| Wound Type | Infection Risk | Mortality if Infected |
|-----------|---------------|----------------------|
| Superficial cut/laceration | 20-30% | Low (~10-20%) unless complications |
| Deep laceration, muscle exposure | 40-60% | Moderate (~30-50%) |
| Penetrating abdominal wound | 70-90% | Very high (~60-80%) - peritonitis |
| Gunshot through bone | 60-80% | High (~50-70%) |
| Crush injury/deep tissue damage | 70-90% | Very high (~60-80%) |
| Joint involvement | 80-90% | Extreme (~70-90%) - septic arthritis |
| Contaminated (dirt, debris) | +25-40% above baseline for wound type | Significantly increased mortality |

### Factors That Worsen Outcomes (All Periods)
1. **Delay in treatment**: Each day untreated = exponentially higher infection rate
2. **Foreign material** in wound (clothing, dirt, arrowheads): Major risk multiplier
3. **Poor nutrition/scurvy**: Immune system compromised; wounds fail to heal
4. **Extreme temperatures**: Cold slows healing; heat accelerates bacterial growth
5. **Multiple concurrent infections**: Sepsis from one wound can kill from another
6. **Age and prior health**: Young healthy = better outcomes; elderly/diseased = worse

### Factors That Improve Outcomes (Pre-Modern)
1. **Prompt cleaning** with wine or clean water: Significant benefit
2. **Honey application**: One of the most effective pre-modern antiseptics
3. **Rest and nutrition**: Critical for immune function
4. **Wound opening/drainage**: Abscesses that drain spontaneously often survive; sealed abscesses frequently kill via sepsis
5. **Amputation within 24 hours** (for limb wounds): Dramatically better than delayed amputation

## 7. Key Historical Turning Points for Game Design

### Timeline of Wound Treatment Evolution

| Era | Technology/Belief | Impact on Wound Survival |
|-----|------------------|-------------------------|
| **Ancient Egypt** (~3100 BCE) | Honey, wine, surgical drainage | Moderate improvement over no treatment |
| **Classical Greece** (~460-370 BCE) | Hippocratic methods: clean water/wine, observation | Systematic approach; moderate survival gains |
| **Roman Era** (Galen, 2nd century CE) | Codified humoral theory; ligatures for bleeding | Improved hemorrhage control; pus-promotion hurt infection outcomes |
| **Islamic Golden Age** (800-1200 CE) | Advanced wound care; catgut sutures; clinical observation | Better documentation; some antiseptic practices |
| **Medieval Europe** (500-1400 CE) | Barber-surgeons; "laudable pus" dogma dominant | Stagnation in infection understanding |
| **Hugh of Lucca** (~1200 CE) | First rejection of pus theory; dry wound treatment | Ahead of his time but not widely adopted |
| **Ambroise Pare** (1530s-1590) | Replaced boiling oil with soothing dressings for gunshot wounds | Significant pain reduction; better outcomes |
| **John Hunter** (1728-1793) | Scientific approach to inflammation and infection | Understanding that surgery could cause harm via infection |
| **Lister/Antiseptic Surgery** (1867+) | Carbolic acid, sterile technique | REVOLUTIONARY improvement; mortality dropped dramatically |

## 8. Summary Numbers for Game Mechanics

### Quick Reference - Pre-Antibiotic Combat Wound Outcomes

- **Simple cut with no treatment**: ~30% infection rate; if infected, ~25% chance of death from sepsis
  - **Net survival: ~72%** (70% not infected + 30% x 75% survive infection)
  
- **Deep wound with dirt contamination, no treatment**: ~60% infection rate; if infected, ~60% chance of death
  - **Net survival: ~36%** (40% not infected + 60% x 40% survive infection)

- **Gunshot limb wound, no treatment**: ~75% infection rate; if infected, ~70% chance of death
  - **Net survival: ~22.5%** (25% not infected + 75% x 30% survive infection)

- **Gunshot limb wound, amputated within 24 hours (Napoleonic era)**: ~50% mortality from surgery/sepsis
  - **Net survival: ~50%**

- **Gunshot limb wound, honey + wine treatment**: Estimated ~40% infection rate; if infected, ~50% chance of death
  - **Net survival: ~60%** (better than no treatment, worse than amputation for severe wounds)

### Post-Antibiotic Era (for comparison/modern settings)
- All wound types: <5% infection rate with basic care
- Septic mortality even if infected: ~15-30%
- **Net survival**: 95%+ for most wounds, 80-90% for severe combat injuries
